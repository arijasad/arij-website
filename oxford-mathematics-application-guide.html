Applying for Mathematics at Oxford.

Choose this course because you want to explore mathematics in depth. Preparation should help you reason with unfamiliar ideas, not collect application credentials.

By Arij Asad, Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer and STEP examinerChecked 9 November 2026

For applications in autumn 2026, entry in 2027 (or deferred entry in 2028). Checked 9 November 2026. The October application and test deadlines have passed. If you are in Year 12 now, recheck the rules for your own entry year before booking tests or choosing subjects.

Important change for 2027 entryOxford Mathematics uses TMUA in the 2026 application round. MAT is no longer held. Old MAT questions remain useful mathematical practice, but they are not a description of the current test.

Entry requirements and Further Maths

The standard A-level offer is A*A*A, with A*s in Mathematics and Further Mathematics where the latter is available. Oxford formally describes Further Maths as highly recommended and normally expects applicants with access to take it. The distinction between unavailable provision and choosing not to study it matters.

If full A-level Further Maths is unavailable, Oxford publishes alternatives involving AS Further Maths or Maths without Further Maths. Ask about your circumstances and ensure your school's provision is explained. Do not read these alternatives as a general invitation to drop Further Maths. The department's offer page gives the detail.

The central course page lists IB 39 including core points, with 766 at Higher Level and 7 in Higher Level Mathematics. Other qualifications have their own requirements. Check the relevant qualification guidance rather than relying on an informal conversion. Physics and Computer Science are not required for the single-subject Mathematics course.

Does Oxford Maths prefer four A levels?

The current departmental prospectus says that taking four does not change the standard offer. There is no automatic admissions bonus for adding a subject. Oxford's broader guidance acknowledges that extra subjects can show workload capacity but warns against spreading yourself too thinly.

If your three subjects already meet the requirements, use the available time well. Deepening Further Maths, tackling unfamiliar questions and discussing an argument can be more useful than carrying an unwanted fourth subject. If you enjoy four and remain on top of the work, there is no need to drop one simply to follow a formula.

The current test: TMUA, not MAT

TMUA has two 75-minute papers, with 20 multiple-choice questions in each. It tests application of school mathematics and mathematical reasoning, including elementary logic. It is computer based, without a calculator. There is no penalty for an incorrect answer.

Oxford required the October sitting, held on 12 to 16 October 2026. The January sitting is not an alternative for the ordinary Mathematics course. Registration for the test was separate from submitting UCAS. Check UAT-UK's dates and eligibility for your own cycle.

The Oxford TMUA guidance says results inform shortlisting alongside UCAS information and school background. It does not publish a pass mark. A historical MAT average cannot be converted into a reliable TMUA offer target, and there is no score that makes the interview irrelevant.

Use the official specification and computer-based practice, then the TMUA archive. Oxford's own preparation livestream and TMUA flashcards are free university resources.

A sensible Year 12 to Year 13 plan

Begin by making the mathematics you already know more flexible. If a quadratic looks unfamiliar after a change of notation, more timed papers will not necessarily repair the underlying difficulty.

  • Year 12: add a regular session of non-routine problems to schoolwork. Explain solutions, vary assumptions and test counterexamples. One well-reviewed problem can generate several useful follow-up questions.
  • Summer: compare your knowledge with the TMUA specification. Use a small diagnostic set, then work on the weaknesses it reveals. Introduce timing in short sets.
  • Early Year 13: build mixed and timed practice around school priorities. Retain some unseen official papers for a realistic later check and spend time reviewing decisions, not just totals.
  • After the test: practise mathematical conversations and reread the interests mentioned in your application. Follow the college's interview instructions.

These are teaching suggestions rather than a prescribed number of hours. My TMUA study plan can be adapted to your starting point. Do not sacrifice sleep or A-level understanding to complete an arbitrary paper count.

Oxford maths interview preparation

Oxford describes its interviews as largely mathematical: you might explore a problem, sketch a curve or work with a new definition. The aim is to understand how you think. Interviews for this cycle are online in December; your invitation gives the exact arrangements.

Practise making your reasoning available to another person. Say what a calculation is intended to establish. If your conjecture fails, use that information. When given a definition, try a small example before making a general claim. Listen to the interviewer instead of continuing a rehearsed explanation after the question changes.

The department explicitly advises against feeling that special admissions training is necessary. A teacher, friend or family member can help you practise explaining. A mathematically knowledgeable partner can also ask follow-up questions. Use official demonstrations to understand the discussion, not to predict its questions.

Do Oxford Maths applicants need STEP?

STEP is not part of the standard Oxford Mathematics admissions route. The current required test is TMUA. Cambridge's usual STEP offer conditions should not be imported into an Oxford plan.

Selected STEP questions can still be useful enrichment once the content is accessible. Their extended arguments develop skills that a multiple-choice answer alone does not show. Use STEP resources selectively if they serve a learning purpose, while prioritising the requirements of all your actual university choices.

Supercurriculars and the current personal statement

Read or explore something because it interests you, then do some mathematics with it. For example, after reading about a result, work through a simple case, explain an assumption or see what fails without it. That gives you more to discuss than a list of book titles.

The UCAS statement now uses three questions, covering motivation, preparation through study and relevant preparation beyond formal education. The format and character limits are explained on the hub. Oxford wants truthful, thoughtful academic engagement. No separate written work is required for this course.

The normal application deadline for 2027 entry was 15 October 2026 at 18:00 UK time. Your reference, predicted grades and relevant circumstances are part of the application too. A polished statement cannot repair missing subject requirements, and a consultant should not write it for you.

Common avoidable mistakes

  • Preparing for MAT as though it were still the required assessment.
  • Interpreting “Further Maths highly recommended” as “unimportant when available”.
  • Trying to display university-level vocabulary without secure school mathematics.
  • Counting books or competitions instead of reflecting on the ideas encountered.
  • Judging an interview entirely by whether you needed hints.

Free preparation and targeted help

You can build a complete independent plan using UAT-UK, Oxford's free materials, the topic guides and historical MAT questions. Keep historical materials matched to the current specification; longer MAT questions can also support discussion practice.

My approach to specialist support is to inspect your attempts first. Persistent difficulty with implication, algebra or choosing a representation calls for different follow-up work. TMUA tuition may help when that diagnosis is missing, but effective support should develop independence rather than turn every question into a guided exercise.

Questions students and parents ask

Is Oxford using MAT or TMUA for 2027 entry?

TMUA. The change applies to the autumn 2026 application round. MAT papers on this site are labelled as historical practice.

Can I apply if my school does not offer Further Maths?

Oxford publishes alternative offers for this situation. Read the department's guidance and explain the provision available to you. Choosing not to take an available course is a different situation.

Is there a TMUA score that guarantees an interview?

Oxford does not publish a guaranteed pass mark. It considers the test alongside the rest of the application and school background.

Will four A levels produce an easier offer?

The Mathematics department says the same standard offer applies. Choose a manageable programme that meets the subject requirements and leaves room to think seriously about mathematics.
