Course guide

Applying for Computer Science at Cambridge.

The mathematical requirements, TMUA and college assessments, plus ways to explore algorithms and prepare for interviews.

By Arij Asad, Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer and STEP examiner

For applications in autumn 2026 for entry in 2027, or deferred entry in 2028.

Mathematics is centralFor 2027 entry, Cambridge Computer Science requires TMUA. Further Maths is required at AS or A level if your school offers it. Some colleges also use CSAT. A large coding portfolio does not replace these requirements.

Entry requirements and school subjects

The published minimum A-level offer is A*A*A. Mathematics is required, with AS or A-level Further Mathematics if your school offers it. For the IB, the minimum is 41 to 42 points overall, with Higher Level grades of 7, 7 and 6, including Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches. Colleges can specify additional details, so use the current course page and college requirements together.

If your school does not offer Further Maths, contact the colleges you are considering. Explain what your school offers and ask which additional mathematics they recommend.

Computer Science A-level is not a stated required subject. It can be useful preparation, but you can apply without it. Check other qualifications and subject combinations individually rather than assuming that strong programming experience compensates for any gap.

Does Cambridge Computer Science need four A levels?

Cambridge normally bases offers on three A levels and says taking four does not usually give an advantage. However, its subject-choice guidance usually expects Further Maths as a fourth for Computer Science when the school permits four. An offer based on three grades does not necessarily mean the college expects you to study only three subjects.

If only three are possible, or a fourth would create a significant difficulty, seek advice from the colleges you are considering. Keep the required mathematics central. Do not drop Further Maths merely to preserve Computer Science as a subject, and do not add an unrelated fourth just to increase the count without considering the workload.

TMUA and college-specific CSAT

All applicants take TMUA. It consists of two 75-minute papers with 20 multiple-choice questions each, without a calculator. The content concerns applying mathematical knowledge and reasoning, rather than programming syntax. There is no penalty for wrong answers.

The October sitting for this round was 12 to 16 October 2026. January is not an alternative for ordinary October applicants. Test booking is separate from UCAS; read the official timetable and exceptions. Cambridge considers the result alongside the rest of your application; there is no universal pass mark.

The current Cambridge course page lists CSAT, an additional Computer Science aptitude test, for Peterhouse and Trinity applicants. Those colleges arrange it and provide instructions. Recheck the list for your cycle and do not assume TMUA is the only possible assessment at every college.

Use the TMUA archive and official preparation materials. STEP is not listed as the standard Computer Science admissions requirement. Selected extended maths questions may help your reasoning, but that does not create a need to prepare for the full STEP examination.

What to prioritise in Year 12 and Year 13

Strong preparation combines mathematics with thoughtful exploration of computing. When you study an algorithm, ask why it works, which inputs might cause problems and how its running time changes as the input grows.

  • Year 12: prioritise understanding your Maths and Further Maths lessons. Add manageable work on unfamiliar problems, logic or a small programming investigation. Keep notes on the methods you tried and the mistakes you learned from.
  • Summer: use the TMUA specification to diagnose gaps. Work on algebra, functions and precise reasoning before increasing time pressure. Keep a project small enough to understand properly.
  • Early Year 13: introduce mixed timed TMUA work and review it carefully. Reserve some unseen official material for later checks. Prepare the application around real interests.
  • After TMUA: practise discussing mathematical and algorithmic problems, review anything mentioned in the application and follow any CSAT instructions.

A useful starting point is one problem-solving session each week and another to review your attempts or explore a computing idea. Adjust the amount of work around your school commitments.

What Computer Science interviews assess

The department's interview guidance describes mathematical problems and, in some discussions, algorithms, logic or programming. It emphasises explaining an approach and responding to help. The aim is not simply to recognise familiar questions or complete everything without a hint.

Practise with a small unfamiliar problem. Define the objects carefully, test a few cases, explain a proposed method and look for a counterexample. If you claim an algorithm always terminates or produces the right result, be prepared to say why. You do not need to turn the conversation into a display of advanced terminology.

For a project you mention, explain the part you wrote, the design decisions and an example of debugging or changing your mind. A teacher or friend can ask useful follow-up questions. Do not rehearse a script that stops working as soon as the assumptions change.

How much do projects and supercurriculars matter?

Cambridge's Computer Science exploration guidance recognises the value of modest projects, including simple command-line programs. A glossy app is not a requirement. What makes an activity useful is the thinking it prompts.

Make a small project more thoughtful

If you implement a search or sorting procedure, compare two approaches on small examples. Identify which operations become expensive. Test an edge case that breaks your first version, then explain the repair. Make sure you can explain each part of your investigation.

Reading, puzzles and mathematical exploration can also be valuable. You do not need to do everything. Choose a few activities you can explore honestly rather than accumulating certificates or copying a sophisticated project you do not understand.

UCAS and My Cambridge Application

The current UCAS statement uses three questions. Explain why the academic subject interests you, how study has prepared you and what relevant exploration you have undertaken. See the current format and limits. Explain which parts of a project you wrote yourself and which came from a tutorial or other source.

Cambridge emphasises academic reflection rather than an activity list and does not formally score the statement. Most applicants also complete My Cambridge Application. For this cycle its usual deadline was 22 October 2026, following UCAS on 15 October. Use the optional extra statement only if you have relevant information to add to your UCAS answers.

Common mistakes

  • Treating Computer Science as a software portfolio competition and neglecting mathematics.
  • Missing the Further Maths qualification or a college's additional CSAT.
  • Practising logic only by recognising familiar answer patterns.
  • Describing a project in terms you cannot explain or defend.
  • Assuming January TMUA can replace the required October sitting.

Free resources for independent preparation

Use official TMUA materials, the TMUA study plan, logic and proof guidance and negation and contrapositive practice. The topic guides provide worked examples and hints when a mathematical weakness becomes clear.

Questions students and parents ask

Do I need Computer Science A-level?

No. Mathematics is required, and AS or A-level Further Maths is required if your school offers it. Check any additional conditions from your chosen college.

Do all colleges require CSAT?

No. The current course page lists Peterhouse and Trinity. Check the current college information and your invitation because arrangements can change.

Should I have a large programming portfolio?

There is no requirement for a large portfolio. A small project that you can explain, test and improve is a more useful learning activity than an impressive-looking product you cannot account for.

Is STEP required for Cambridge Computer Science?

It is not the standard requirement on the current course page. TMUA is required, with CSAT at the specified colleges. Always read the conditions of your individual offer.
