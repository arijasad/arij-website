## Points requiring careful interpretation

1. Cycle naming: “2026/27” means applications in autumn 2026 for entry in 2027, not an application submitted in autumn 2027. October deadlines are already past. Each page warns current Year 12 readers to recheck their own cycle.
2. Oxford Mathematics now uses TMUA, not MAT. Oxford Engineering Science now uses ESAT, not PAT. Some official departmental FAQ headings retain old terminology; current central course pages and current test guidance take precedence.
3. Cambridge Mathematics requires both TMUA and normally STEP 2 and STEP 3. TMUA is not a replacement for STEP. Flexible STEP offers are individual/college-specific and still require both papers under the published arrangement.
4. Four A levels: Cambridge Maths recognises three-subject applicants and school context. Cambridge Engineering and CS usually expect Further Maths as a fourth where four are available. Oxford normally bases offers on three; an extra subject is not an automatic advantage.
5. Further Maths: Oxford Maths normally expects it where available, despite the formal “highly recommended” label. Oxford Engineering explicitly does not require it. Cambridge requirements and school-access caveats are stronger and course-specific.
6. Cambridge Engineering: Peterhouse may add STEP 2. Cambridge CS: Peterhouse and Trinity currently add CSAT. These are not universal requirements for all colleges.
7. Cambridge central 2027 Maths guidance and faculty pages are not fully consistent on IB totals and flexible-offer college lists. The published guide uses the central 41 to 42 minimum, avoids reproducing an inconsistent college list and tells readers to check their own offer.
8. Interviews: Oxford current arrangements are online; Cambridge formats vary by college. No fixed number, exact duration or universal schedule is promised. College invitations govern associated tasks and equipment.
9. No guaranteed test cutoff, invented selection weighting or probability of admission is published. Historical MAT, PAT, ENGAA and TMUA results are not presented as current thresholds.
10. UCAS uses three questions, with a 350-character minimum for each and 4,000 characters overall. My Cambridge Application is a separate process. Test booking is also separate from UCAS.
11. Weekly preparation suggestions, reserving unseen papers and criteria for targeted tutoring are Arij’s teaching judgement, clearly separated from official requirements.
12. “Usually no general agency is needed” is an argued practical recommendation based on free official guidance and access, not a measured universal claim about admissions outcomes.
13. Free independent resources are distinguished from assessment entry fees. No claim is made that sitting the tests is free.

## SEO rationale

Each course page serves a distinct informational query; comparison pages address a separate decision intent; the consultancy guide answers a practical parent question. Course pages use descriptive headings and realistic FAQs such as four A levels, Further Maths and current tests. Internal links connect discovery through archives to learning guidance and, where useful, relevant tuition. Advice stands independently from service descriptions.

Pages are static, crawlable HTML with unique titles, meta descriptions, self-canonical URLs, Article/CollectionPage and BreadcrumbList structured data, visible authorship, official sources and a clear application year. All nine are in the sitemap. No promise of rankings or FAQ rich results is made, and FAQ schema is not added merely to seek a rich result.

Assess performance by page/query impressions and clicks and by genuine enquiries, not pageviews alone. The existing consent-based attribution can identify advice-origin journeys after publication. This release does not create a new tracking service or automatic monitoring schedule.
