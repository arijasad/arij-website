The college interview process

Expect more than one conversation.

01 / Invitation

Your first college

Shortlisted applicants receive online interview invitations. The first interviewing college can differ from your original choice, including after reallocation or an open application.

Oxford’s interview guidance
02 / Mathematical discussion

About 25 minutes

The Maths department describes generally one or two interviews at the first college, each about 25 minutes. Expect unfamiliar problems, graphs or a new definition to explore.

Mathematical Institute guidance
03 / Another college

A normal part of the process

The department describes at least one interview at another college. Exact arrangements vary, so use each invitation to confirm the time, format and any preparation.

College interview structure

Most Maths interviews are scheduled for 14 to 17 December 2026; some colleges start in the week beginning 7 December. Your invitation takes priority. Check Oxford’s 2026 timetable.

Make your thinking visible.

Try an example, explain a possible approach and say where you are unsure. The discussion gives tutors a way to see how you reason and develop an idea.

If you receive a hint, pause to understand what it changes. A useful practice session includes time to reflect on why one approach worked and another stalled.

For Mathematics and Computer Science or Mathematics and Philosophy, expect discussion covering both subjects. Read the department prospectus.

Check your online setup.

Mathematics is listed under Oxford’s tier 3 setup: Microsoft Teams, a Miro whiteboard and a touchscreen with a stylus for handwriting.

Test writing equations and drawing diagrams in advance. If suitable equipment is unavailable, contact the college; Oxford says applicants and schools are not expected to buy a touchscreen device.

Official examples & practice

Free resources worth spending time on.

Choose a few problems and give them a proper attempt. A demonstration is useful for understanding the discussion, rather than as a performance to memorise.

  • Oxford sample interview questions

    Open the Mathematics section for examples and explanations from tutors. These are practice examples, not advance questions for your interview.

  • Mathematics demonstration interview

    Choose Mathematics (tier 3). Watch the discussion and tutor commentary; a transcript is also available.

  • Oxford Online Maths Club

    Recorded problem-solving discussions. Pause at a question, try an approach, then compare it with the discussion.

  • Maths livestreams & worksheets

    Choose the interview sessions and worksheets to practise discussing a problem and explaining your ideas.

  • Versatility worksheet (PDF)

    A problem-solving worksheet including a past Oxford interview question. Try it independently, then talk through your reasoning.

  • Versatility worksheet solutions

    Use after an independent attempt, then try to reconstruct the reasoning without the solution in front of you.

A few practical questions.

Is there a pre-interview maths paper?

The current central maths guidance reviewed does not specify a single timed preparation paper for all applicants. Follow your college’s invitation for any task or material. A missing public description does not establish that there is no task.

Oxford interview FAQs

Do I need specialist interview training?

Oxford says special admissions training is not required. Working on mathematics and discussing it with a teacher or study partner are useful ways to prepare. Individual tuition is optional.
