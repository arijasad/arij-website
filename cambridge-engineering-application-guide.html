Applying for Engineering at Cambridge.

The useful preparation is mathematical and physical: build a model, explain an assumption and work out what changes when the situation changes.

By Arij Asad, Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer and STEP examinerChecked 9 November 2026

For applications in autumn 2026, entry in 2027 (or deferred entry in 2028). Checked 9 November 2026. The October application and test deadlines have passed. If you are in Year 12 now, recheck the rules for your own entry year before booking tests or choosing subjects.

Start with the right subjects and modulesCambridge Engineering requires Mathematics and Physics, with Further Maths expected where available. The admissions test is ESAT, taking Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. Do not assume that the subject or module choices are interchangeable with those for another quantitative course.

Entry requirements and subject expectations

The 2027 course page publishes a minimum A-level offer of A*A*A. Mathematics and Physics are required, together with AS or A-level Further Mathematics if your school offers it. Colleges may specify where the A*s must fall and can set additional conditions. IB guidance lists 41 to 42 with 776 at Higher Level, including Higher Level Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches and Physics. Colleges can require higher totals or particular 7s; ask for advice if Analysis and Approaches is unavailable.

If Further Maths is unavailable, Cambridge recommends additional pure mathematics and mechanics and discussion with prospective colleges. This is a reason to investigate accessible provision, not to assume that your application is impossible or that no further preparation is needed. For IB, other international qualifications or an unusual teaching sequence, check the full course and college requirements before choosing subjects.

Three or four A levels: the important qualification

Four A levels are not automatically better than three. However, Cambridge's subject-choice guidance usually expects Further Maths as a fourth subject for Engineering when the school permits four. The general statement that offers are usually based on three subjects does not cancel that advice.

Where a school permits only three, Pembroke's Engineering guidance recommends Maths, Physics and Further Maths. That is useful college-specific guidance, not a promise that every college handles every combination identically. Check your prospective colleges before dropping a subject, especially if Further Maths is taught in Year 13 after an early Maths A level.

The decision should consider access, grades and workload. An extra subject taken solely to look impressive can crowd out the mathematical fluency needed for the course. Equally, removing Further Maths without checking the consequences can close an important preparation route.

ESAT format, timing and use

Engineering applicants take Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. Each ESAT module contains 27 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes, giving 120 minutes across the three. The test is computer based at an authorised centre, with no calculator and no deduction for wrong answers.

The ordinary Cambridge application round used 12 to 16 October 2026. The January 2027 sitting is not a second chance for that round; UAT-UK specifies limited exceptions for other application routes. Test booking is separate from UCAS. See the ESAT guidance and cycle timetable.

ESAT reports a separate score for each module. There is no universal pass mark, and Cambridge considers assessment performance alongside other application evidence. An old ENGAA score or a community mock conversion is not a reliable admissions threshold.

Use the official specification and practice environment first. The ESAT archive also contains historical ENGAA, NSAA and PAT material, clearly separated from current ESAT practice. Select older questions for their content rather than assuming that a whole old paper reproduces the new test.

How much preparation is sensible?

I would organise preparation around the mathematical decisions that keep causing difficulty. Can you turn a diagram into equations? Can you estimate before calculating? Can you tell whether an answer has the right units and behaviour?

  • Year 12: prioritise school Maths and Physics, including algebra, graphs and mechanics. Add one or two short sessions of unfamiliar problems each week if the workload allows. Include time to review, not just attempt.
  • Summer: check every ESAT topic against what you have studied. Use a small diagnostic set, repair gaps and begin short timed work. Keep a record of whether lost marks came from knowledge, interpretation, calculation or timing.
  • Early Year 13: move towards mixed module practice and realistic timings. Revisit slow correct answers as well as errors. Preserve some unseen official material for later checks.
  • After ESAT: practise explaining mathematical and physical reasoning in a discussion. Schoolwork remains important throughout.

This is my suggested starting point, not a university hours requirement. If additional work makes core subjects deteriorate, adjust it. Starting early should create stronger reasoning, not months of repeated full mocks.

What engineering interviews are assessing

Cambridge's interview guidance emphasises applying knowledge to unfamiliar situations and thinking independently while responding to new ideas. For Engineering, useful preparation includes mathematical modelling and physical explanation. Interview arrangements vary by college, so follow the invitation rather than assuming all candidates have the same format.

A useful way to practise

Take a mechanics problem you have not seen. Draw the system, identify the forces, state what you are neglecting and predict what happens if a mass or length doubles. Ask a partner to change one assumption. Explain whether the model still works.

This is a suggested practice activity, not a claim about an actual interview question.

Practise accepting a hint and using it. If you are unsure, say which step is uncertain. Rehearsing a perfect description of why you love engineering is less useful than becoming comfortable revising a flawed argument.

Does Cambridge Engineering require STEP or TMUA?

ESAT is the standard pre-interview test for Engineering, not TMUA. STEP is not a general Engineering requirement, but there are college exceptions: Cambridge notes that Peterhouse may require grade 2 in STEP 2. Check the college guidance and any individual offer.

Do not build an entire STEP programme simply because another Engineering applicant had that condition. If STEP is relevant to your route, the preparation timeline and STEP resources explain how to begin.

Supercurriculars, projects and UCAS

A small investigation can be worthwhile: model an everyday mechanism, compare a prediction with measurements, or explore why an approximation fails. You should be able to explain the assumptions, the result and the next question. Expensive equipment or a prestigious placement is not necessary for that.

Cambridge Engineering does not require industrial experience before starting the degree. Do not confuse later course requirements with an admissions checklist. If you do have relevant experience, explain what you learned technically rather than relying on the organisation's name.

The current UCAS statement uses three questions. Link motivation, academic study and wider exploration with specific reflection. See the current UCAS format. Most applicants also needed My Cambridge Application, normally due on 22 October 2026 after the 15 October UCAS deadline. Check any college requests separately.

Common mistakes

  • Booking the wrong ESAT modules or treating January as a general resit.
  • Assuming a four-subject rule is identical for Cambridge Maths and Engineering.
  • Doing only maths practice while leaving Physics weak.
  • Using a memorised formula without checking its assumptions or units.
  • Spending more time presenting a project than understanding it.

Free resources and when teaching helps

Use UAT-UK's free preparation alongside I Want to Study Engineering, which Cambridge recommends, and Isaac Science Physics. The maths topic guides can help repair particular mathematical weaknesses before you return to a test question.

Specialist support is most useful when it addresses an identifiable need: translating words into a model, choosing an efficient method, or explaining why a physical argument is valid. My teaching starts with the student's attempt and uses hints and related problems to develop that skill. A tutor should be clear about the mathematical and physical areas they can support, and you should still do substantial independent work.

Questions students and parents ask

Can I apply to Cambridge Engineering with three A levels?

Possibly, depending on subjects, school provision and college expectations. Maths, Physics and Further Maths is specifically recommended by Pembroke where only three can be taken. If four are available, Cambridge's expectation about Further Maths as a fourth needs checking.

Which ESAT modules do I take?

Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics for the current Engineering course. Do not use the more flexible module rules for another course.

Do I need engineering work experience?

It is not required before entry. Relevant experience can help you explore the subject, but so can accessible problems, reading and a modest investigation you understand.

Should I prepare for STEP as well?

Check your college. It is not the general requirement, although Peterhouse may set STEP 2. Avoid adding a major extra assessment to your plan without a reason.
