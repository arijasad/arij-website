Do you need an admissions consultancy?

Usually, I would start without a general admissions agency. First establish what the course requires, what you can do independently and whether there is a specific difficulty that needs help.

By Arij Asad, Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer and STEP examinerChecked 9 November 2026

For applications in autumn 2026, entry in 2027 (or deferred entry in 2028). Checked 9 November 2026. The October application and test deadlines have passed. If you are in Year 12 now, recheck the rules for your own entry year before booking tests or choosing subjects.

My starting adviceMost students should begin with the official guidance, school support and a manageable independent plan. A general admissions agency is usually unnecessary for accessing information or organising preparation. If a specific learning difficulty remains, consider focused help for that difficulty.

What the universities actually say

Cambridge states that commercial interview services are not endorsed and do not have exclusive information. Oxford's Mathematics department says applicants should not feel they need special admissions training and recommends mathematical practice and explanation. Both provide free official guidance.

My conclusion is that a general package should not be treated as a prerequisite. That is practical advice, not a claim that no student ever benefits from support. A family unfamiliar with UK applications may value help organising the process, and a student may benefit from excellent subject teaching. Those are specific services, not privileged access to admission.

I offer specialist tuition myself. I would still want a student to know what they can do independently and why any proposed lesson would be useful. A useful plan should work even if you never book with me.

What you can organise independently

  1. Choose and check the course. Read the official course structure, subjects and qualification requirements. Use the course guides to organise the questions, then verify details at source.
  2. Make one calendar. Include school deadlines, UCAS, separate test booking, access arrangements, college forms and any later offer conditions. Do not assume the UCAS submission completes everything.
  3. Build a preparation routine. Start with school understanding and a small number of unfamiliar problems. Review the obstacles that actually recur.
  4. Explore the subject. Read, investigate or build something modest. Keep notes on the ideas and questions it generates.
  5. Write your own application. Use specific reflection in the current UCAS format. Ask for clarity checks without replacing your voice or inventing experiences.
  6. Practise discussion. A teacher, peer or family member can help you explain an argument. Where possible, include someone able to challenge the mathematics.

For a parent, useful support can be as simple as protecting quiet study time, helping with logistics and listening without turning every practice score into a prediction. Ask what the student has learned and what they will try next.

A free preparation starting point

  • UAT-UK's official materials for TMUA and ESAT specifications, samples and the test environment.
  • Cambridge's STEP Support Programme for progressive assignments, hints and solutions.
  • Oxford Maths interview guidance and Cambridge interview preparation.
  • The site's free archives and preparation guides, topic practice and challenge problems.

Free does not mean unstructured. Choose a sequence, keep a record of attempts and revisit difficult ideas. Check eligibility for university outreach or school-based support rather than assuming paid help is the only way to get feedback.

Start with learning, not an endless mock schedule

In Year 12, a regular session on a substantial problem and time to review it can be a useful beginning. During the summer and early Year 13, make preparation more test-specific where required. The STEP timeline and TMUA plan give adaptable sequences.

I would not use every available official paper as soon as possible. Once you remember a solution, the paper becomes a less honest check of unfamiliar problem solving. Keep some unseen material for later, while using selected older questions and topic work to build the underlying skills.

There is no universal weekly hour target. School understanding, health and the quality of review should shape the workload. A late burst of drilling cannot reliably replace months of developing mathematical judgement.

When specialist tutoring can add value

Support is easier to judge when the need is concrete. Examples include persistent algebra errors, difficulty interpreting logical statements, incomplete STEP arguments, poor time allocation or little experience of explaining unfamiliar mathematics aloud.

A productive lesson might begin with your unsuccessful attempt. The tutor identifies the exact obstruction, offers a small hint and checks whether you can use it. A related problem then shows whether the idea has transferred. Over time, you should need less prompting.

That differs from having someone narrate solutions while you watch. Before committing to a long programme, establish what is being addressed and how you will recognise progress. You may need occasional diagnostic feedback, a short sequence of focused lessons or ongoing support; the answer should follow the learning need.

My TMUA tuition, STEP teaching and Cambridge interview preparation are described separately so you can assess their scope. You do not need a full package simply because you want help with one topic.

Questions to ask before paying

  • What specific difficulty will the work address?
  • Will the tutor look at my independent attempts and give feedback on reasoning?
  • What work will I do between sessions, and why?
  • Are the course and test rules current and linked to official sources?
  • Is the tutor clear about their expertise, fees and cancellation arrangements?
  • Can we review whether the support is still useful?

Be cautious about guaranteed offers, claims of exclusive questions, pressure to buy large bundles or statements written on the student's behalf. A past interviewing role does not give someone access to a future decision or make their teaching a university-endorsed service.

What paid help cannot replace

It cannot choose your academic interests for you, make an unread book part of your understanding or turn a memorised answer into independent reasoning. It should not take ownership of your application or project.

Good help may make practice more focused and feedback more useful. The student still needs to attempt problems, reflect and make decisions. The aim is to arrive better able to learn mathematics, not merely better able to perform preparation routines.

Questions students and parents ask

Can I get into Cambridge or Oxford without paid preparation?

Paid preparation is not an entry requirement. Both universities provide free guidance and subject resources. A school teacher, peer or independent plan can provide much of what you need, with targeted help considered only where useful.

Will an agency know what the interviewers want?

Official guidance describes the academic qualities being assessed. Commercial providers do not have exclusive official information or control over decisions. Judge support by the quality of its teaching, not suggestions of privileged access.

How many mock interviews should I buy?

There is no necessary number. Start by practising a real mathematical discussion and use the feedback. Another session is useful if it checks an improvement or addresses a remaining difficulty, not just because a package includes it.

Is a paid summer school or impressive work experience necessary?

Neither is a universal requirement for these courses. Check the individual course guidance. Accessible reading, problems and modest investigations can demonstrate meaningful academic engagement when you understand and reflect on them.

Should someone rewrite my personal statement?

Someone can help you notice unclear passages or unsupported claims. The experiences, reflections and writing should remain your own. The current three-question format gives a useful structure without needing a manufactured story.
