UCAS application advice

UCAS application advice.

Course requirements, admissions tests and practical preparation for students and parents applying to Cambridge or Oxford.

By Arij Asad, Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer and STEP examiner

For applications in autumn 2026 for entry in 2027, or deferred entry in 2028.

These guides explain the course requirements and how to prepare for admissions tests and interviews. They are written for students and parents applying for Maths, Engineering and Computer Science.

My advice draws on my experience as a Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer, STEP examiner and maths tutor. I focus on understanding where you get stuck, working through the difficulty and then trying a related problem independently.

The October application and test deadlines have passed. If you have applied for 2027 entry, the next stage is to follow your college’s instructions and prepare for any interview while keeping up with schoolwork. The dates below belong to this application round.

In China, Hong Kong and Macau, ESAT took place on 12 to 13 October and TMUA on 15 to 16 October. The January sitting is not an alternative for the standard Oxford or Cambridge application round. Exceptions apply to eligible applicants through Cambridge’s mature-college January round and Oxford Foundation Year routes. You can take each test only once per application cycle.

Read the official test timetable for booking, access-arrangement and bursary deadlines, including any regional differences. Request access arrangements or an eligible bursary before booking. A UCAS submission does not book a test. STEP has a separate examination-centre registration process, covered in the Cambridge Maths guide.

The current UCAS personal statement

The personal statement has three questions: why you want to study the subject, how your studies have prepared you, and what you have done outside formal education to prepare. Each answer must contain at least 350 characters. The total limit is 4,000 characters, including spaces. Read UCAS's exact questions and guidance.

Choose a few concrete examples and explain what changed in your understanding. Avoid repeating the same material across answers. Write in your own words, and do not claim an activity or insight that you cannot discuss. The course guides explain how to make the academic content relevant.

Turn the advice into a useful practice session

  1. Choose a problem that uses topics you have studied but needs an approach you have not seen before.
  2. Attempt it before looking at help. Record what you tried and where progress stopped.
  3. Use a small hint, then continue. Explain why the resulting method works.
  4. Try a related question later without the hint.

Start with topic practice, the algebra diagnostic or maths challenge problems. For examination planning, use the TMUA study plan, STEP timeline and interview guide. You do not need to work through every resource to benefit from them.

A few starting questions

Does this guidance apply to students in Year 12 now?

The preparation advice is useful in Year 12. The dates and requirements here are for applications made in autumn 2026, so students applying in a later year should use the official guidance for their own entry year.

Are four A levels always better?

No. The required subjects matter more than the number alone. Cambridge also gives specific advice about taking Further Maths as a fourth subject for Engineering and Computer Science when your school allows it. See the relevant course guide.

Are these guides official university advice?

No. They are independent guides written by Arij Asad, with links to official requirements. Preparation suggestions are teaching advice, and individual college instructions take precedence.
