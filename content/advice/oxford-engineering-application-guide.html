For 2027 entryOxford Engineering Science uses ESAT, with Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. PAT is historical practice, not the current required test. Further Maths is helpful but is explicitly not required for admission.

Entry requirements and Further Maths

The published A-level requirement is A*A*A including Mathematics and Physics. The two A*s must be in Mathematics, Physics or Further Mathematics. Oxford lists IB 40 including core points, with 776 at Higher Level and 7s in Mathematics and Physics. Use the department's entry requirements for the precise qualification route.

The central course page states that Further Maths can help with the degree but is not an admission requirement. If it is available and manageable, it can deepen useful mathematical preparation. If it is unavailable, that does not itself make you ineligible. Maths, Physics and Further Maths can also form a valid three-A-level combination.

Students taking other qualifications should check Oxford's accepted-equivalents guidance. Where disruption or other relevant circumstances affect your record, ensure the application explains them through the appropriate route. Do not assume that a statement of enthusiasm changes the standard academic offer.

Do four A levels improve an Engineering application?

Oxford does not require four for Engineering Science. Its general guidance says offers normally use three and warns against sacrificing grades or wider academic engagement. It also recognises that additional subjects can demonstrate workload capacity. The sensible conclusion is that four are not automatically better, rather than that an extra subject can never be useful.

Consider what the fourth subject costs and contributes. If it develops an interest while your Maths and Physics remain secure, it may be worthwhile. If it creates constant catch-up, the time could be better spent understanding the subjects the course actually requires.

ESAT: the current admissions test

The three required modules each have 27 multiple-choice questions and a 40-minute time limit. That is 120 minutes in total. ESAT is computer based, with no calculator and no penalty for wrong answers. It reports separate module scores rather than one combined course-specific pass mark.

Oxford required the October sitting on 12 to 16 October 2026. The ordinary Engineering Science course cannot use January as an alternative. Test registration was separate from UCAS. Current and future applicants should check UAT-UK's timetable, including support and booking deadlines.

The Engineering department says it considers all available evidence for shortlisting and final decisions, without fixed weightings. Neither a single ESAT score nor a strong interview provides a guarantee. Avoid claims that a historical PAT result converts into a particular ESAT threshold.

Start with official ESAT materials. The ESAT practice archive keeps current samples separate from older PAT, ENGAA and NSAA papers. Those older questions are useful only when matched to the present content and purpose.

Preparation in Year 12 and Year 13

For Engineering, understanding and fluency need to develop together. A student may know Newton's laws yet struggle to decide which forces belong in a model. Another may model correctly but lose time manipulating the resulting equations. These require different practice.

  • Year 12: make school Maths and Physics reliable. Add manageable sessions of unfamiliar problems and discussion. Draw diagrams, check dimensions and explain the meaning of a graph's shape.
  • Summer: read the current ESAT specification. Use short diagnostic sets to separate missing content from slow reasoning, then practise the specific weaknesses.
  • Early Year 13: introduce mixed timed work and module pacing. Keep some official material unseen for later practice. Review errors before attempting another paper.
  • After ESAT: practise discussing problems and responding to a changed assumption. Keep schoolwork and the interests in your application active.

I would begin with one or two additional focused sessions a week in Year 12 if schoolwork is secure, then adjust according to progress. That is a teaching suggestion, not an Oxford requirement. The quality of the review matters more than accumulating hours.

What the interview is trying to find out

Expect an academic discussion involving mathematical and physical reasoning. Oxford's current course guidance says shortlisted candidates have online interviews in December. Your college invitation determines the schedule and working arrangements.

Prepare to explain a model, not just quote an answer. State what is known, choose a representation and justify the assumptions. If an interviewer changes a parameter, consider its effect before calculating. If given a hint, use it and show how the argument develops.

A useful practice discussion might start with a graph or mechanics question and continue with “Why does that sign make sense?” or “What would happen in an extreme case?” Practise with a teacher or a partner who can ask follow-up questions. Repeating the same mock until the answers sound smooth removes much of its learning value.

Do STEP or TMUA play a role?

Neither STEP nor TMUA is listed as the standard Oxford Engineering Science admissions requirement. ESAT is the assessment to prioritise. Check your other university choices separately because test requirements can differ.

A selected extended maths problem can still develop useful reasoning, but you do not need a full STEP programme solely for this application. Similarly, historical PAT questions should be treated as extra practice rather than an examination you are preparing to sit.

Projects, supercurriculars and UCAS

The four-year Engineering Science degree begins with broad engineering foundations. Read about that actual course before deciding that a particular specialism or job title is your only reason for applying.

Useful exploration could be a simple measurement, a small design comparison, a technical article or a simulation that you understand. Explain what the result depended on and what you would investigate next. A costly summer programme or placement is not a stated admission requirement.

For the current three-question UCAS statement, connect your interests with evidence from study and relevant exploration. See the format and limits. Do not use a list of activities to hide a lack of reflection. There is no separate written-work submission for this course.

The 2027-entry UCAS deadline was 15 October 2026 at 18:00 UK time. Your academic reference, grades, test and interview evidence all matter. Ask your school to explain relevant circumstances rather than trying to package them as a persuasive story.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Following an old PAT timetable or overlooking ESAT module choices.
  • Assuming Oxford's Further Maths policy is identical to Cambridge's.
  • Practising calculations without explaining their physical meaning.
  • Inventing a fixed weighting or guaranteed score for selection.
  • Choosing a prestigious-looking project that you cannot discuss in detail.

Independent work and targeted support

Use UAT-UK's specification and samples, Oxford's official interview guidance, Isaac Science Physics and I Want to Study Engineering. Work on mathematical weaknesses with the topic-practice guides, then return to a question that uses the idea in context.

Specialist tuition can help when repeated attempts do not reveal the underlying issue, or when you have little opportunity for a challenging discussion. Ask for feedback on your reasoning and a clear purpose for follow-up work. The aim should be to need fewer prompts over time, not to outsource preparation.

Questions students and parents ask

Is Further Maths required for Oxford Engineering?

No. Oxford explicitly calls it helpful but not required. Maths and Physics are required, and the grade conditions still apply.

Is PAT still used for Engineering Science?

No for the 2026 application round leading to 2027 entry. The required assessment is ESAT, with Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics.

Can Maths, Physics and Further Maths be my three A levels?

Yes, that combination meets the stated subject requirements. Check that you can meet the full grade conditions and the requirements of your other university choices.

Does a project need to be technically advanced?

I would prioritise a modest project you can explain and question. A complicated product does not show much about your reasoning if you cannot account for how it works.
