For 2027 entryOxford Engineering Science uses ESAT, with Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. Older PAT papers can still be used for practice, but applicants now take ESAT. Further Maths is helpful but is explicitly not required for admission.

Entry requirements and Further Maths

The published A-level requirement is A*A*A including Mathematics and Physics. The two A*s must be in Mathematics, Physics or Further Mathematics. For the IB, Oxford requires 40 points including core points, with Higher Level grades of 7, 7 and 6. The two 7s must be in Mathematics and Physics. Use the department's entry requirements for the precise qualification route.

The central course page states that Further Maths can help with the degree but is not an admission requirement. If it is available and manageable, it can deepen useful mathematical preparation. If it is unavailable, that does not itself make you ineligible. Maths, Physics and Further Maths can also form a valid three-A-level combination.

Students taking other qualifications should check Oxford's accepted-equivalents guidance. If disruption has affected your studies, ask your school how to explain this in your application.

Do four A levels improve an Engineering application?

Oxford does not require four for Engineering Science. Its general guidance says offers are normally based on three subjects. A fourth can show that you can manage a demanding workload, but it should not come at the expense of your grades or time to explore Engineering.

Consider whether you enjoy the fourth subject and can manage the extra work. If it develops an interest while your Maths and Physics remain secure, it may be worthwhile. If it creates constant catch-up, the time could be better spent understanding the subjects the course actually requires.

ESAT: the current admissions test

The three required modules each have 27 multiple-choice questions and a 40-minute time limit. That is 120 minutes in total. ESAT is computer based, with no calculator and no penalty for wrong answers. You receive a separate score for each module.

Oxford required the October sitting on 12 to 16 October 2026. Applicants for the standard Engineering Science course cannot use the January sitting instead. Test registration was separate from UCAS. Current and future applicants should check UAT-UK's timetable, including support and booking deadlines.

The Engineering department says it considers all available evidence for shortlisting and final decisions, without fixed weightings. Neither a single ESAT score nor a strong interview provides a guarantee. Avoid claims that a historical PAT result converts into a particular ESAT threshold.

Start with official ESAT materials. The ESAT practice archive keeps current samples separate from older PAT, ENGAA and NSAA papers. Choose older questions that practise topics in the current ESAT specification.

Preparation in Year 12 and Year 13

For Engineering, understanding and fluency need to develop together. A student may know Newton's laws yet struggle to decide which forces belong in a model. Another may model correctly but lose time manipulating the resulting equations. These require different practice.

  • Year 12: make school Maths and Physics reliable. Add manageable sessions of unfamiliar problems and discussion. Draw diagrams, check dimensions and explain the meaning of a graph's shape.
  • Summer: read the current ESAT specification. Try a short set of questions to see which topics you have not learned and which you understand but answer slowly. Plan your practice around those gaps.
  • Early Year 13: introduce mixed timed work and module pacing. Keep some official material unseen for later practice. Review errors before attempting another paper.
  • After ESAT: practise discussing problems and responding to a changed assumption. Keep up with schoolwork and revisit any projects or reading mentioned in your application.

I would begin with one or two additional focused sessions a week in Year 12 if schoolwork is secure, then adjust according to progress. Adjust this to your workload. Reviewing a difficult question carefully is more useful than rushing through more papers.

What the interview is trying to find out

Expect an academic discussion involving mathematical and physical reasoning. Oxford's current course guidance says shortlisted candidates have online interviews in December. Your college invitation determines the schedule and working arrangements.

Prepare to explain a model, not just quote an answer. State what you know, draw a diagram or write equations, and explain any assumptions you make. If an interviewer changes a parameter, consider its effect before calculating. If given a hint, use it and show how the argument develops.

A useful practice discussion might start with a graph or mechanics question and continue with “Why does that sign make sense?” or “What would happen in an extreme case?” Practise with a teacher or a partner who can ask follow-up questions. Repeating the same mock until the answers sound smooth removes much of its learning value.

Do STEP or TMUA play a role?

Neither STEP nor TMUA is listed as the standard Oxford Engineering Science admissions requirement. ESAT is the assessment to prioritise. Check your other university choices separately because test requirements can differ.

A selected extended maths problem can still develop useful reasoning, but you do not need a full STEP programme solely for this application. Similarly, historical PAT questions should be treated as extra practice rather than an examination you are preparing to sit.

Projects, supercurriculars and UCAS

The four-year Engineering Science degree begins with broad engineering foundations. Read the course outline to see which areas you would study before specialising.

Useful exploration could be a simple measurement, a small design comparison, a technical article or a simulation that you understand. Explain what the result depended on and what you would investigate next. A costly summer programme or placement is not a stated admission requirement.

For the current three-question UCAS statement, connect your interests with evidence from study and relevant exploration. See the format and limits. For each activity you mention, explain what you learned or found interesting. There is no separate written-work submission for this course.

The 2027-entry UCAS deadline was 15 October 2026 at 18:00 UK time. Your academic reference, grades, test and interview evidence all matter. Ask your school to explain any circumstances that affected your studies.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Following an old PAT timetable or overlooking ESAT module choices.
  • Assuming Oxford's Further Maths policy is identical to Cambridge's.
  • Practising calculations without explaining their physical meaning.
  • Inventing a fixed weighting or guaranteed score for selection.
  • Choosing a prestigious-looking project that you cannot discuss in detail.

Free resources for independent preparation

Use UAT-UK's specification and samples, Oxford's official interview guidance, Isaac Science Physics and I Want to Study Engineering. Work on mathematical weaknesses with the topic-practice guides, then return to a question that uses the idea in context.

Questions students and parents ask

Is Further Maths required for Oxford Engineering?

No. Oxford explicitly calls it helpful but not required. Maths and Physics are required, and the grade conditions still apply.

Is PAT still used for Engineering Science?

No for the 2026 application round leading to 2027 entry. The required assessment is ESAT, with Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics.

Can Maths, Physics and Further Maths be my three A levels?

Yes, that combination meets the stated subject requirements. Check that you can meet the full grade conditions and the requirements of your other university choices.

Does a project need to be technically advanced?

A small project can give you plenty to discuss. Be ready to explain how it works, what went wrong and what you would improve.
