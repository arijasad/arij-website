The current UCAS personal statement

The format introduced for 2026 entry remains relevant here: three responses, at least 350 characters each, within 4,000 characters overall including spaces. The prompts concern your reasons for studying the subject, preparation through education and useful preparation beyond formal education. Read UCAS's exact questions and guidance.

Choose a few concrete examples and explain what changed in your understanding. Avoid repeating the same material across answers. Write in your own words, and do not claim an activity or insight that you cannot discuss. The course guides explain how to make the academic content relevant.

Turn the advice into a useful practice session

  1. Choose a question that is accessible in content but unfamiliar in approach.
  2. Attempt it before looking at help. Record what you tried and where progress stopped.
  3. Use a small hint, then continue. Explain why the resulting method works.
  4. Try a related question later without the hint.

Start with topic practice, the algebra diagnostic or maths challenge problems. For examination planning, use the TMUA study plan, STEP timeline and interview guide. You do not need to work through every resource to benefit from them.

A few starting questions

Does this guidance apply to students in Year 12 now?

The learning advice can, but a Year 12 student in November 2026 will usually be preparing for a later entry year. The requirements here are checked for the autumn 2026 application round. Recheck the next cycle's official information before choosing or booking assessments.

Are four A levels always better?

No. Course and school context matter, especially Cambridge's Further Maths expectations for Engineering and Computer Science. Each course guide explains its own position.

Are these guides official university advice?

No. They are independent guides written by Arij Asad, with links to official requirements. Preparation suggestions are teaching advice, and individual college instructions take precedence.
