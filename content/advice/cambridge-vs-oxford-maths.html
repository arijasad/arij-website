Compare the mathematics you would study

Both courses develop rigorous mathematical thinking across pure and applied areas. Cambridge offers a Mathematics with Physics option in the first year and later mathematical specialisation. Oxford has separate joint routes including Mathematics and Computer Science and Mathematics and Philosophy; their specific requirements need checking separately.

Do not reduce the choice to “Cambridge is applied, Oxford is pure” or the reverse. Read the actual first-year content, later options and progression rules. Look for topics you would enjoy studying throughout the degree.

A useful comparison exercise is to choose two areas you enjoy, such as probability and mechanics, then inspect how each degree develops them. Also look at an area you currently find difficult. Consider both the topics you already enjoy and the areas you would like to understand better.

TMUA is shared; STEP changes the later workload

Older advice often contrasted Oxford's MAT with Cambridge's STEP. That no longer describes the full 2027-entry route: both now use TMUA before interview, while Cambridge normally also uses STEP in its conditional offers.

This matters when planning the spring of Year 13. Cambridge applicants normally need to prepare for STEP’s longer written problems alongside A levels. An Oxford applicant should check other university choices before deciding whether STEP is relevant at all.

Oxford’s lack of a standard STEP condition does not make it an easier course to enter. Both universities assess your mathematical ability. Choose the course that interests you, then make time for its required assessments.

Three or four A levels?

For Mathematics, neither university's guidance supports an automatic advantage from adding a fourth subject. Cambridge emphasises school context, and Oxford keeps the same standard Maths offer for students taking four. Further Maths is the more important question to resolve.

If an additional subject would weaken the required mathematics or leave no time to explore unfamiliar problems, reconsider it with your school. If you take Maths early, contact prospective colleges about the remaining workload and offer conditions. Avoid turning another student's experience into a universal rule.

Are the interviews very different?

At both universities, prepare for a mathematical conversation. Practise explaining your reasoning, working with unfamiliar ideas and using hints. The precise arrangement depends on the course and college; Oxford's current interviews are online, while Cambridge arrangements vary.

Much of the preparation is useful for both: explain why a method works, test your ideas with examples and try another approach when the first one fails. The Cambridge interview guide explains these habits, but its logistical details should not be applied to Oxford.

A practical way to decide

  1. Read both official course structures and mark the topics or options that genuinely interest you.
  2. Check your qualifications and subject access against the course and college guidance.
  3. Compare the assessment workload, including the role of STEP.
  4. Use open days or official recordings to ask about teaching, feedback and ordinary student life.
  5. Make a choice you can explain without reference to a league table.

Parents can help by discussing the course options, keeping track of deadlines and making space for regular preparation.

Questions students and parents ask

Can I apply to Oxford and Cambridge Maths in the same year?

Not normally in the same undergraduate admissions round. Check official eligibility rules for any exceptional route rather than assuming you can submit both choices.

Does Cambridge still require STEP now that it uses TMUA?

Yes, normal Maths offers still include STEP 2 and STEP 3. Read your college's exact conditions and any published alternatives.

Is Oxford Maths easier to get into because there is no STEP?

That does not follow. The selection stages differ, and headline applicant ratios do not tell you an individual student's probability of admission. Choose by course fit and prepare for the relevant route.

Can I use the same preparation resources initially?

Yes. Secure school mathematics, unfamiliar problems and TMUA practice are useful for both. Cambridge applicants also need a sustained plan for STEP. Use official specifications to decide when the preparation should diverge.
