Start with the right subjects and modulesCambridge Engineering requires Mathematics and Physics, with Further Maths expected where available. The admissions test is ESAT, taking Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. These are the three modules required for Engineering.

Entry requirements and subject expectations

The 2027 course page publishes a minimum A-level offer of A*A*A. Mathematics and Physics are required, together with AS or A-level Further Mathematics if your school offers it. Colleges may specify which subjects need an A* and can set additional conditions. For the IB, the minimum is 41 to 42 points overall, with Higher Level grades of 7, 7 and 6, including Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches and Physics. Colleges can require higher totals or particular 7s; ask for advice if Analysis and Approaches is unavailable.

If Further Maths is unavailable, Cambridge recommends additional pure mathematics and mechanics and discussion with prospective colleges. Ask the colleges what further study they recommend and whether you can access it through your school or another provider. For IB, other international qualifications or an unusual teaching sequence, check the full course and college requirements before choosing subjects.

Do I need three or four A levels?

Four A levels are not automatically better than three. However, Cambridge's subject-choice guidance usually expects Further Maths as a fourth subject for Engineering when the school permits four. This expectation can still apply even when the offer itself is based on three subjects.

Where a school permits only three, Pembroke's Engineering guidance recommends Maths, Physics and Further Maths. Other colleges may give different advice. Check your prospective colleges before dropping a subject, especially if Further Maths is taught in Year 13 after an early Maths A level.

Discuss your subject choices with your school and prospective colleges. Consider which courses are available, the grades you are likely to achieve and the time you need for Maths, Physics and Further Maths.

ESAT format, timing and use

Engineering applicants take Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. Each ESAT module contains 27 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes, giving 120 minutes across the three. The test is computer based at an authorised centre, with no calculator and no deduction for wrong answers.

For the standard Cambridge application round, ESAT took place on 12 to 16 October 2026. The January 2027 sitting is not a second chance for that round; UAT-UK specifies limited exceptions for other application routes. Test booking is separate from UCAS. See the ESAT guidance and cycle timetable.

ESAT reports a separate score for each module. There is no universal pass mark, and Cambridge considers assessment performance alongside other application evidence. An old ENGAA score or a community mock conversion is not a reliable admissions threshold.

Use the official specification and practice environment first. The ESAT archive also contains historical ENGAA, NSAA and PAT material, clearly separated from current ESAT practice. Choose older questions that cover the current ESAT topics. Their original timings and paper structure differ from ESAT.

How much preparation is sensible?

I would organise preparation around the mathematical decisions that keep causing difficulty. Can you turn a diagram into equations? Can you estimate before calculating? Can you tell whether an answer has the right units and behaviour?

  • Year 12: prioritise school Maths and Physics, including algebra, graphs and mechanics. Add one or two short sessions of unfamiliar problems each week if the workload allows. Include time to review, not just attempt.
  • Summer: check every ESAT topic against what you have studied. Use a small diagnostic set, repair gaps and begin short timed work. Keep a record of whether lost marks came from knowledge, interpretation, calculation or timing.
  • Early Year 13: move towards mixed module practice and realistic timings. Revisit slow correct answers as well as errors. Preserve some unseen official material for later checks.
  • After ESAT: practise explaining mathematical and physical reasoning in a discussion. Schoolwork remains important throughout.

Adjust the amount of practice around your schoolwork. Starting early gives you time to understand difficult ideas before adding the pressure of full timed papers.

What engineering interviews are assessing

Cambridge's interview guidance emphasises applying knowledge to unfamiliar situations and thinking independently while responding to new ideas. For Engineering, useful preparation includes mathematical modelling and physical explanation. Interview arrangements vary by college, so follow the invitation rather than assuming all candidates have the same format.

A useful way to practise

Take a mechanics problem you have not seen. Draw the system, identify the forces, state what you are neglecting and predict what happens if a mass or length doubles. Ask a partner to change one assumption. Explain whether the model still works.

This is a suggested practice activity, not a claim about an actual interview question.

Practise accepting a hint and using it. If you are unsure, say which step is uncertain. Practise recognising when an approach is not working and explaining what you would try next.

Does Cambridge Engineering require STEP or TMUA?

ESAT is the standard pre-interview test for Engineering, not TMUA. STEP is not a general Engineering requirement, but there are college exceptions: Cambridge notes that Peterhouse may require grade 2 in STEP 2. Check the college guidance and any individual offer.

Do not build an entire STEP programme simply because another Engineering applicant had that condition. If STEP is relevant to your route, the preparation timeline and STEP resources explain how to begin.

Supercurriculars, projects and UCAS

A small investigation can be worthwhile: model an everyday mechanism, compare a prediction with measurements, or explore why an approximation fails. You should be able to explain the assumptions, the result and the next question. Expensive equipment or a prestigious placement is not necessary for that.

Cambridge Engineering does not require industrial experience before starting the degree. Do not confuse later course requirements with an admissions checklist. If you do have relevant experience, explain what you learned technically rather than relying on the organisation's name.

The current UCAS statement uses three questions. Explain why Engineering interests you, how your studies have prepared you and what you have learned from exploring it outside lessons. See the current UCAS format. Most applicants also needed My Cambridge Application, normally due on 22 October 2026 after the 15 October UCAS deadline. Check any college requests separately.

Common mistakes

  • Booking the wrong ESAT modules or treating January as a general resit.
  • Assuming a four-subject rule is identical for Cambridge Maths and Engineering.
  • Doing only maths practice while leaving Physics weak.
  • Using a memorised formula without checking its assumptions or units.
  • Spending more time presenting a project than understanding it.

Free resources for independent preparation

Use UAT-UK's free preparation alongside I Want to Study Engineering, which Cambridge recommends, and Isaac Science Physics. The maths topic guides can help repair particular mathematical weaknesses before you return to a test question.

Questions students and parents ask

Can I apply to Cambridge Engineering with three A levels?

It depends on your subjects, what your school allows and your chosen college’s requirements. Maths, Physics and Further Maths is specifically recommended by Pembroke where only three can be taken. If your school allows four, Cambridge usually expects Further Maths as a fourth subject.

Which ESAT modules do I take?

Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics for the current Engineering course. Do not use the more flexible module rules for another course.

Do I need engineering work experience?

It is not required before entry. Relevant experience can help you explore the subject, but so can accessible problems, reading and a modest investigation you understand.

Should I prepare for STEP as well?

Check your college. It is not the general requirement, although Peterhouse may set STEP 2. Avoid adding a major extra assessment to your plan without a reason.
