Course guide

Applying for Mathematics at Cambridge.

How A-level choices, TMUA, interviews and STEP fit together, with suggestions for preparation in Year 12 and Year 13.

By Arij Asad, Cambridge Mathematics graduate, former Cambridge interviewer and STEP examiner

For applications in autumn 2026 for entry in 2027, or deferred entry in 2028.

The route for 2027 entryCambridge Mathematics uses TMUA to help decide whom to interview. Offers normally also require grades in STEP 2 and STEP 3, taken the following summer. TMUA has not replaced STEP.

Entry requirements and Further Maths

The published minimum A-level offer is A*A*A, with Mathematics and Further Mathematics required. The normal STEP condition is grade 1 in each of STEP 2 and STEP 3. Colleges decide individual offers and may specify the subjects in which the A*s are needed. For the IB, the 2027 course page gives a minimum of 41 to 42 points overall, with Higher Level grades of 7, 7 and 6, including Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches.

Meeting the grade requirements does not guarantee an offer. If your school does not offer Further Maths, contact the colleges you are considering to discuss your circumstances and how you could study the required material.

Physics is not required for the standard Mathematics course. For Mathematics with Physics, you need to have studied mechanics through Further Maths or Physics, alongside the required Maths and Further Maths. Some colleges may also require Physics. For other qualifications, early A-levels, resits or unusual combinations, use Cambridge's qualification guidance and ask the college rather than translating grades yourself.

Do I need four A levels for Cambridge Maths?

Four A levels are not automatically better than three. The Mathematics faculty FAQ says that applicants taking three are not disadvantaged and that offers normally involve three A levels plus STEP. It also says school context matters: where most pupils take four, the college may expect an explanation for taking three.

A fourth subject can be worthwhile if you enjoy it and can manage the extra work. It is less helpful if it leaves too little time for Further Maths, problem solving or your required grades. Discuss your school's pattern and any proposed subject change with your teachers and prospective college before deciding.

TMUA: the test before interview

For this cycle, TMUA took place on 12 to 16 October 2026. It is computer based at a test centre: two 75-minute papers, each containing 20 multiple-choice questions. One concerns applying mathematical knowledge; the other emphasises reasoning and logic. Calculators are not permitted and incorrect answers do not lose marks. See UAT-UK's current format.

The faculty application guidance describes TMUA as an input to interview shortlisting. It is considered alongside the rest of your application. Do not treat an old raw-mark conversion or an online target score as a guaranteed invitation. Applicants in the standard October round must take TMUA in October. The January sitting is only available for specific application routes listed by UAT-UK.

Use the current specification before choosing practice. The TMUA archive separates official material, university support and community mocks. Its historical score information is not a current admissions cutoff.

STEP: the exams taken after an offer

STEP asks for written mathematical arguments. Each current paper lasts three hours and contains 12 questions; the best six responses count. STEP 2 draws on A-level Maths and AS Further Maths, while STEP 3 also uses A-level Further Maths. The OCR specification is the authority for the precise content.

OCR lists STEP 2 on 14 June 2027 and STEP 3 on 21 June 2027, with registration through an examination centre from 1 March to 4 May. Check the official dates and speak to your school's exams officer well before the deadline.

Some colleges use alternative or flexible offer conditions. This does not mean every applicant can replace a STEP grade with an extra A*. Published flexible arrangements still require both papers to be taken. The conditions in your own offer are what matter; ask the college if anything is unclear.

What to do in Year 12 and Year 13

I would begin with regular, manageable mathematical work rather than full mock papers. The free STEP Support Programme provides an excellent progression from foundation problems to STEP 2 and STEP 3.

  • Year 12: make algebra, functions and trigonometry reliable. Try a foundation assignment or one substantial unfamiliar problem each week, with a second session to revisit your reasoning. Learn to write a complete argument.
  • Summer before applying: check the TMUA specification, diagnose gaps and introduce short timed sets. Continue some longer problems so that speed does not become your only measure of progress.
  • Early Year 13: balance TMUA practice with schoolwork. Review wrong answers and slow correct answers. Keep some unseen official material for later checks.
  • After TMUA: explain unfamiliar problems aloud, listen to hints and practise changing direction. Continue working on longer STEP problems alongside your other preparation.
  • After an offer: plan which STEP topics to study and when to practise under timed conditions, alongside your A levels. Practise choosing questions as well as solving them.

Adjust this plan to what you need. If algebra is still causing difficulty, spend time on that before attempting several full STEP questions each week. The existing when to start STEP guide goes into more detail.

What the interview is assessing

The central activity is working on mathematics with an interviewer. The faculty describes looking at how you think, make connections and respond when an idea is unfamiliar. If you get stuck, the interviewer may offer a hint and see how you use it. Needing a hint does not mean the interview has gone badly.

Prepare by asking someone to discuss a problem you have not already solved. Explain why you chose a substitution, what a diagram suggests or which case might disprove your claim. When given a hint, explain how it helps you move forward. It is fine to pause and think; you do not need to describe every arithmetic step aloud.

Some colleges also arrange written work as part of the interview process. Read your college's invitation for its format, any associated assessment and equipment requirements. Use the Cambridge maths interview guide and problems with hints for practice. Avoid collecting purported recent interview questions or memorising polished solutions.

Supercurriculars and the UCAS application

Supercurricular activities are ways of exploring mathematics beyond your lessons. That might be a proof you tried to repair, a STEP problem you generalised or a chapter whose argument you worked through. You do not need to turn every interest into a certificate. Competitions can supply good problems; a medal is not a substitute for understanding.

The current UCAS statement has three questions. Use them to explain your mathematical interests, preparation through study and relevant exploration beyond lessons. The current format and limits are summarised on the advice hub. Cambridge reads the statement but does not give it a formal score.

Most applicants also complete My Cambridge Application. Its usual deadline in this cycle was 22 October 2026. The optional extra statement is useful when you have something Cambridge-specific to add, not a reason to repeat your UCAS answers. Let your referee explain relevant school limitations and circumstances.

Mistakes worth avoiding

  • Assuming TMUA has removed the need for STEP.
  • Using every recent paper before you can learn much from a timed sitting.
  • Reading a solution as soon as a question becomes uncomfortable.
  • Reading advanced topics while leaving gaps in A-level Maths or Further Maths.
  • Using another student's college offer as if it were a universal rule.

Free resources for independent preparation

Start with the official specifications, STEP Support Programme, STEP papers and solutions, and topic practice with hints. Attempt a problem, record where you got stuck, use the smallest helpful hint and return to it later without the solution.

Questions students and parents ask

Can I apply with three A levels?

Yes. Cambridge Maths says applicants taking three A levels are not disadvantaged, provided they meet the subject requirements. If most pupils at your school take four, or you take Maths early, ask your prospective colleges before changing subjects.

Should I wait for an offer before starting STEP?

I would start the underlying problem solving in Year 12 and develop it steadily. An offer is a reason to make the plan more systematic, not necessarily the first time to attempt an extended problem.

Does a high TMUA score remove the STEP condition?

No automatic exemption is published. TMUA and STEP have different roles. Follow the actual conditions issued by your college.

Do I need Olympiad medals or research experience?

Neither is listed as an entry requirement. Use activities to deepen your mathematics, and describe what you actually understood rather than trying to assemble an impressive list.
