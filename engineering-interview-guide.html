Engineering interviews.

What to expect at Cambridge and Oxford, how to practise, and a few good places to find problems.

Updated 18 November 2026

What to expect ↓Preparation advice ↓Practice resources ↓

What happens in the interview?

Expect a discussion involving maths and physics. Tutors want to see how you work through a problem and develop an idea when they give you some guidance.

My preparation advice

Make your reasoning easy to follow.

  1. Draw the situation. Label the quantities, forces or circuit components. A clear diagram often reveals the first useful step.
  2. Explain your model. Say which physical principle you are using and what you are assuming. For example, are you ignoring friction or treating a body as a particle?
  3. Check what the answer means. Look at the units, sign and size. Consider an extreme case, or ask what would happen if a mass, length or voltage doubled.
  4. Use a hint. If you get stuck, explain where. Listen to the suggestion, try it, and say what it changes in your approach.
  5. Know your own examples. Revisit projects, reading and interests from your application. Be ready to explain the engineering involved and what you learned.

For practice, choose an unfamiliar problem and talk it through with someone who can ask follow-up questions. Afterwards, identify the step that caused difficulty and try a related problem.

What should I revise?

Start with the maths and physics you have already studied. Useful areas to revisit include algebra, graphs, calculus, forces, moments, energy and circuits. Practise connecting equations to a physical situation.

What do I need for an online interview?

Oxford lists Engineering under its tier 3 setup: Microsoft Teams, a Miro whiteboard and a touchscreen with a stylus. Practise writing equations and drawing diagrams. Contact the college if suitable equipment is unavailable.

For Cambridge, follow your college’s instructions about how to share your working.

Practice resources

A few useful starting points.

  • Oxford’s sample questions

    Open the Engineering section for questions and tutor commentary. The department’s engineering example (PDF) explores forces and the stability of a dam.

  • Cambridge demonstration interview · Oxford demonstration interview

    Watch how the student and tutors develop the discussion. Pause when a problem is introduced and think about what you would try.

  • I Want to Study Engineering

    Maths, physics and engineering problems with hints. Try a problem independently before using the help.

  • Isaac Science Physics

    Use targeted physics problems to strengthen an area you find difficult, then practise explaining the reasoning aloud.
